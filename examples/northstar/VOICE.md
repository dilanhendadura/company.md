# Northstar Cloud voice context

## Voice Principles

1. **Operational before aspirational.** Show the job, boundary, and next step before describing a future.
2. **Calm under consequence.** State risk plainly without drama or reassurance theatre.
3. **Specific enough to test.** Prefer an observable action over an abstract benefit.
4. **Respect the accountable operator.** Never imply that governance is friction to remove {claim:voice.operator-register}.

## How We Talk

Use direct sentences, concrete nouns, and active verbs. Explain one mechanism before summarizing its benefit. Sound like an experienced operator walking a peer through a controlled change: candid about limits, interested in evidence, and economical with emphasis.

## How We Never Sound

Never sound like a futurist keynote, a fear-driven security vendor, a chatty consumer assistant, or a consultancy hiding uncertainty behind nouns. Avoid breathless transformation claims and false certainty.

## Phrases We Use

- “Name the job, boundary, and approval.”
- “Show the evidence behind the action.”
- “Start with one workflow that matters.”
- “A promising prototype is not yet an operating capability.”

Use these only when they advance the argument; they are not slogans to paste into every asset.

## Phrases We Avoid

- “Revolutionize” → describe the changed workflow.
- “Seamless” → name the integration and remaining handoff.
- “Single pane of glass” → state what becomes visible and to whom.
- “AI-powered” → name the agent's bounded job.
- “Eliminate human error” → describe the control, test, or review.

## Writing Mechanics

Use sentence case for headings. Keep paragraphs to one claim or mechanism. Expand an acronym at first use. Use numerals for measured quantities and write the measurement window. Prefer a colon to stacked parentheses. Do not use exclamation marks in product, sales, or support copy.

## Channel Adaptations

Product copy is shortest and action-led. Support copy acknowledges the observed state before instructions. Sales copy can lead with commercial consequence but must preserve qualifications. Executive writing begins with the decision and evidence. Social writing may be conversational but keeps the same claims and never manufactures urgency.

## Examples of Good Writing

**Weak:** “Unlock seamless, AI-powered compliance at scale.”

**Good for a product overview:** “Give each agent one job, define the records it can change, and require approval where accountability stays human.”

**Weak:** “Northstar eliminates audit risk.”

**Good for sales follow-up:** “Northstar keeps the procedure, evidence, approvals, and activity history together so your team can review how a case moved.”

## Review Checklist

- Is the task, actor, and boundary concrete?
- Can every factual claim be traced to an approved source?
- Are limitations visible before the call to action?
- Did we replace generic AI language with the customer's operating language?
- Would an accountable operator recognize the workflow?
